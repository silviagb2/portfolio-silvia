CASE STUDY

A platform redesign
driven by users

Fine Dining Lovers is Sanpellegrino's main digital platform — 26 million sessions a year, four languages, audiences across three continents. The challenge wasn't traffic. It was turning readers into registered users.

I joined this project at the start and have been working on it ever since — from the initial discovery phase through UX definition, UI support, and now ongoing product iterations.

PROBLEM

High traffic,
low registrations

The site worked like a magazine: people came, read an article, and left. The registration rate was very low — users had no real reason to create an account. This meant the platform couldn't personalise the experience or retain its audience.

The business goal was clear: turn passive readers into registered users.

OBJECTIVE

Give users a reason
to register

The platform had the audience. What it was missing was a reason for them to create an account. The goal was to identify what value we could offer to logged-in users — and design an experience around it.

Two KPIs measured success:

  • Increase the user registration rate
  • Increase average engagement time — to confirm that users found the new platform more valuable

Defining Features

Understanding what users
actually needed

The research team led the discovery phase across three key markets — US, Italy, and France — to identify what new features would give users a real reason to register. I worked closely with them throughout the process: preparing test materials and scripts, attending sessions to take notes, and joining the analysis of results.

PROCESS

01

Discovery

The team ran user tests to define 3 user personas: Casual Foodie, Active Foodie, and Super Foodie. I supported the preparation of test scripts and materials, and attended the sessions taking notes.

02

Ideation Workshop

With the research insights in hand, we ran a workshop to brainstorm and prioritise features. I contributed to the ideation and helped review ideas against what we'd learned from users.

03

Validation & Testing

The shortlisted features were tested in a quantitative study to confirm which ones had the most impact. The results shaped the final feature set.

UX DEFINITION

Shaping the user
experience

With the features defined, the focus shifted to how the platform would actually work. I led the UX definition together with my UX lead — from benchmarking reference apps to defining the information architecture and building out the wireframes and flows.

Context & premises

Cross-device experience

Built as a PWA with a mobile-first approach, ensuring a smooth experience across all devices while supporting desktop.

User access & personalization

The experience differentiates between non-logged users, who have limited access and are encouraged to sign up, and logged-in users, who get full functionality and personalized content.

Content restructuring

A complete reorganization of the platform's content was necessary, including templates and tagging, to improve discoverability and relevance.

Technical constraints

The platform is built on Drupal, which introduced certain constraints that influenced design decisions — particularly around templating and content structure.

Benchmark

I analysed direct competitors and apps with similar features to identify patterns, best practices, and gaps we could address. The goal was to make informed decisions — understanding what works elsewhere so we could do it better in context.

Sitemap definition

Before touching any screen, we needed to agree on how the platform would be organised. I worked with my UX lead to map out the core sections — defining what lived where and how users would move between them. This became the reference point for everything that followed.

Home

A personalized hub where users discover content, mixing recommendations, saved items, and pending content for a tailored homepage.

Explore

The discovery zone where users can search and explore categorized content such as lists, articles, recipes, and more.

Restaurants

Users can explore restaurants on an interactive map, discovering nearby dining options.

Library

A personal space where users access saved content, including bookmarks and custom restaurant lists.

Profile

Users manage preferences and personalization tags in their profile, designed with future membership integration in mind.

Wireframing & flow definition

With the architecture defined, I started building out the wireframes and flows — screen by screen, state by state. The goal was to define every interaction before touching the visual layer, and to validate the logic with the team before investing time in UI.

We validated these through user tests, iterating based on feedback. My tasks included collaborating with the research team on test planning, creating the question guide, building the prototype, and leading some user tests.

HOME

Personalized for Every User

The Home was designed for two audiences: logged-in users saw personalized content like saved items and tailored recommendations, while non-logged-in users were shown general content mixed with branded messages and CTAs to drive sign-ups.

Key adjustments Post-Test

Made personalized content and actions more evident for logged-in users.

RESTAURANTS

Balancing Information Display

The challenge with the Restaurants page was to create a flexible design that worked well for both restaurants with abundant information and those with less. We adjusted modules to ensure a consistent layout without distortion, no matter the amount of content.

Key adjustments Post-Test

Users found the "Add a note" feature to be valuable, so we moved it to a more prominent location to improve its visibility and ease of access.

TASTE MATCH

Seamless Integration

The "Taste Match" functionality, introduced midway through the project by the creative team, allowed users to give likes or dislikes to content, similar to Tinder, helping the platform understand their preferences. The challenge was integrating it prominently across the app without disrupting user experience.

We added it to various flows, such as the Explore section as a search tool, the Restaurants map for personalized restaurant recommendations, and during the onboarding process to better define the user's profile for content personalization.

Key adjustments Post-Test

Added a progress counter to help users feel more confident and avoid confusion about when the swipe process ended.

UI DEFINITION

Bringing the interface
to life

The visual identity of FDL was being built from scratch. My role here was primarily support and review — working alongside the visual team to flag usability issues, ensure UI decisions held up against what we'd defined in the UX phase, and review accessibility across components and layouts.

Key Contributions

Cards visual design

One of the most complex challenges was designing the content cards. The platform has eight different content types — articles, recipes, videos, podcasts, chefs, destinations, restaurants, and restaurant lists — and the client wanted each one to look visually distinct at a glance, not just different in the information they showed.

I worked closely with the UI team to define the visual logic for each card type, making sure the hierarchy was clear and the designs were consistent enough to feel like a system but different enough to be recognisable.

UI library

I also contributed to building the UI library — the component foundation that kept everything aligned across the platform. That library is now being rebuilt as a full Design System, which is the subject of the other case study in this portfolio.

What I learned

A clear KPI keeps the team focused. Having user registration as the primary metric helped prioritise decisions throughout the project — when in doubt, we'd go back to the question: does this help users register?

Features get users in, but value keeps them. New features drove registration, but the real lesson was that sustainable growth means continuously delivering something worth coming back for.

The project didn't end at launch. Over a year after the initial release, I'm still working on FDL — iterating on the product, implementing client requests through Jira, improving navigation, adding gated content, and building new campaign landing pages. A product is never finished.
